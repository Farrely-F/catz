    Cat training app · plan and design

    Training that starts with your cat, not a lesson list.

    The left side is a clickable prototype of the core loop: build your cat, set the pace, run a lesson, use the clicker. Everything on the right is what I'd build around it, including a few additions to your list.

    What I'd add to your list

    Food motivation and favorite treat in onboardingMVP

    The single biggest lever in clicker training. A picky cat gets a "find the treat" lesson before anything else.

    One parametric cat avatar, used everywhereMVP

    Coat pattern, color, eyes, fur length. The same cat is the mascot: it blinks, follows your finger, hops on a win.

    Clicker with treat budget and soft timerMVP

    Fires on press, not release. Counts treats against a daily budget and ends rounds gently at five minutes.

    "Not today" keeps the streakMVP

    No hearts, no lives, no fail state. Stopping when a cat isn't in the mood is correct training, so it is rewarded.

    Win journal and shareable win cardsMVP

    One line and a photo per win. The share card is the growth loop.

    Vet check before litter box or aggression programsMVP

    Behavior changes often have a medical cause. Gate those programs behind a short checklist.

    Multiple cats, one accountLater

    Per-cat plans and a "separate rooms" reminder. The data model supports it from day one.

    Reminders at the cat's best timeLater

    Uses the "before dinner" answer from onboarding.

    Leaderboards and friend racesSkip

    Competition adds pressure, and pressure is the opposite of how cats learn.

    How the plan adapts

    Onboarding answers are inputs to a small rules function, not decoration. Change the sliders on the left and the Plan screen rewrites itself.

    buildPlan(cat, owner)
  start   = cat.food === 'picky'  ? 'Find the treat she loves'
          : cat.confidence < 40   ? 'Clicker, from a distance'
          :                         'Charge the clicker'
  pace    = confidence < 40 ? 'gentle, soft click'
          : confidence > 65 ? 'quick, fewer repeats' : 'steady'
  rounds  = owner.minutes → 2×2–3 | 2×5 | 3×5
  tricks  = age > 10 ? 'low-impact only'     // no jumps
          : age < 1  ? 'short bursts'
          :            'full list'
  notes  += cat.energy > 65   ? 'play break between rounds'
  notes  += owner.otherCats   ? 'train in separate rooms'

    Rules that keep it fun, not sloppy

    Everything presses

    Buttons, chips and nodes have 4 to 7px of depth and sink when tapped. Springy overshoot on selection.

    The cat reacts

    Eyes follow your pointer, ears flick, tail speed follows the energy slider, a hop on every win.

    Positive only

    No lives, no timers that punish. Rewards for showing up, including rest days.

    Clicker feels instant

    Fires on pointer down with a two-part click-clack, like a real box clicker: a sharp click on press, a duller clack on release. Plus a pulse ring and optional vibration. iOS Safari has no vibration, so the visual pulse carries it.

    The repo already has Next.js 16, Tailwind 4, shadcn and motion. Data stays on the device for the MVP, so there is no backend to run.

    Defaults I picked

    • Name and look are original: no owl, no copied wording from either app.
    • Device-only storage and one cat for the MVP. Accounts and sync come after.
    • Dark text on the orange and mint buttons, since white fails contrast on those colors.
    • Treat budget uses a simple count of 20 until we ask for the cat's weight.
